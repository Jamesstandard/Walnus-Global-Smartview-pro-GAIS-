# SV-006 — Surface Selection & Coating Visualisation

**Milestone:** SV-006  
**Priority:** High  
**Category:** Surface Intelligence / Colour Visualisation

## 1. Project Objective

Introduce practical surface-interaction and coating-visualisation capabilities into SmartView Pro.

The user must be able to capture a building, identify individual surfaces, select them, and apply paint colours or coating finishes to preview the proposed appearance.

The system must preserve the original photograph and keep visualisation separate from actual survey measurements.

**The objective is to make SmartView useful for demonstrating proposed painting work to property owners, engineers, contractors, and clients.**

## 2. Surface Selection Engine

Implement a modular surface-selection system.

Required capabilities:

- Load captured survey photographs.
- Display images in an interactive workspace.
- Allow users to select individual surfaces.
- Support walls, ceilings, and other identifiable coating surfaces.
- Support multiple independently selectable regions.
- Allow selection adjustment.
- Allow adding and removing selected regions.
- Preserve the original image.
- Save selections against the appropriate survey.

### 2.1 Selection Methods

Support a staged implementation:

1. Manual touch-based selection.
2. Boundary-assisted selection.
3. AI-assisted surface identification when technically available.

Manual selection must remain available even when AI services are offline.

AI must not be a mandatory dependency for core selection functionality.

## 3. Coating and Colour Assignment

After selecting a surface, users must be able to assign a proposed coating.

Required functionality:

- Paint colour selection.
- Colour palette.
- Custom colour selection.
- Surface-specific colour assignment.
- Multiple colours on one building.
- Ability to change or remove assigned colours.
- Save colour assignments.
- Preview different combinations.

The system should support future integration with the Walnus Global paint catalogue.

The coating architecture must be modular so that additional paint products, finishes, and manufacturers can be introduced without rebuilding the visualisation engine.

## 4. Realistic Visualisation Engine

The visualisation must preserve the underlying building geometry and architectural details.

Requirements:

- Respect surface boundaries.
- Preserve windows and doors.
- Preserve shadows and structural details.
- Maintain realistic lighting relationships.
- Avoid painting vegetation, roads, and unrelated objects.
- Support different surface finishes.
- Avoid excessive colour bleeding.
- Maintain the original image resolution where possible.

### 4.1 Visualisation Options

Provide two distinct views:

**A. Original Capture**

The untouched photograph captured by SmartView.

**B. Proposed Coating Preview**

A visual representation of the selected surfaces with their assigned colours and finishes.

The original photograph must never be overwritten.

## 5. AI Integration

AI-assisted visualisation must be treated as an enhancement rather than the foundation of the survey system.

AI functionality may include:

- Identifying potential wall boundaries.
- Assisting with surface segmentation.
- Suggesting suitable selection regions.
- Improving visualisation.
- Assisting with colour recommendations.

AI-generated results must be reviewable.

Users must be able to:

- Accept.
- Reject.
- Edit.
- Regenerate.

Maintain version history for visualisation changes.

**Critical restriction:** AI-generated imagery must never become the source of truth for physical measurements, building dimensions, or material quantities.

## 6. Data Persistence

All surface selections and visualisation decisions must be saved against their respective survey records.

Preserve:

- Original photograph.
- Selected surface regions.
- Surface identifiers.
- Assigned colours.
- Coating selections.
- Preview versions.
- User modifications.
- Creation timestamps.

Maintain offline-first behaviour using the established data architecture.

Do not compromise existing survey data structures. Any required schema changes must be versioned, migrated, and tested without loss of existing survey data.

## 7. Modular Architecture

Keep the following components independently replaceable:

1. Surface selection interface.
2. Surface boundary engine.
3. Colour management.
4. Coating catalogue integration.
5. Visualisation renderer.
6. AI-assisted segmentation.
7. Preview history.
8. Local persistence.

The system must allow future integration of more sophisticated image processing and 3D capabilities without requiring a complete rewrite.

## 8. Explicit Exclusions

Do not attempt to introduce the full 3D Studio at this stage.

Specifically defer:

- Full image-to-3D reconstruction.
- Video-to-3D reconstruction.
- Advanced 3D building manipulation.
- Complete 3D material simulation.
- Automated quantity calculations from AI-generated images.

These remain future capabilities.
